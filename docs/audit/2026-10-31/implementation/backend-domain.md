# Backend domain implementation — 31 October 2026

Eleven domain findings are implemented; BD-01 and BD-09 are contained by disabling unsafe entry paths. The current code adds append-only supplier payments, linked customer refunds and credit allocations rather than merely rejecting those operations. No production transfers, refunds, deployments or merchant data repairs were performed.

The final targeted financial suite passed **176 tests in 87.05 seconds**. The subsequent account/party compatibility suite passed **22 tests**, and the final legacy negative-return/handler check passed **17 tests**; these suites overlap. They cover monetary commands, replay and overpayment guards, return credit, shifts, immutable costs, schema upgrades, quote/invoice parity, journal integrity, AI readers and invoice accounts. The root/security agent separately reports 11 passing disposable PostgreSQL role/B2B checks. The canceled older combined run is not counted as passing evidence. Machine-readable details are in `backend-domain-status.json` and `backend-domain-results.json`.

The main financial change is one balance contract shared by money commands, reports, collections, AI responses and printed invoices. It separates original sales from returns, cash receipts from credit applications, refunded cash from available credit, and supplier review status from payment status. Printing retains the original fiscal total while disclosing the return-adjusted payable and actual cash movements. Historical note-only returns are read only when their exact delimited reference identifies one bill in the same business and customer account; new refunds still require structural parent links.

Legacy paid headers have explicit provenance. New authored bills start with a zero legacy baseline; a header-only legacy bill captures its old paid balance once before receiving its first modern receipt. Readers add that baseline to actual receipts. A stale modern header cannot become additional cash, and capture of a legacy baseline never creates a new cash journal. Unknown historical cost snapshots similarly remain unknown: P&L discloses incomplete profit instead of guessing current catalogue cost. Genuine zero-cost snapshots remain zero on returns.

B2B quotes and final invoices use the same tax and rounding calculation, preserve quoted destination and line tax facts, and reject seller tax-state or rounding-policy changes that require a new quote. Buyer–seller commands enter the explicitly validated trade-service transaction under restricted PostgreSQL runtime roles. Returned service-session objects load their lines before the session closes. Order and document party snapshots preserve historical identity through retirement; archival policy and PostgreSQL privilege installation are owned by the root/security work.

The financial schema installer is additive and shared by Alembic and desktop startup. Revision `a30f1d7b9c02` follows `f7b8c9d0e1f2`; subsequent security and archival revisions extend that chain. It creates settlement facts and tenant/immutability constraints, quote policy fields, nullable cost/legacy-payment evidence, and a real existing-schema debit-note unique index. Duplicate historical debit-note numbers cause a visible migration failure for reconciliation rather than silent renumbering.

Five dedicated settlement tests also passed on a newly created disposable loopback PostgreSQL database in **10.56 seconds**, using native advisory locks, real committed second-session replay, period/shift guards, paid-return refund/allocation journals, wrong-tenant rejection, tenant-composite foreign keys and immutable SQL updates. The database was dropped afterward. The PostgreSQL financial CI step now includes this dedicated file; ordinary SQLite runs skip it. These proofs complement the separate restricted-role B2B tests rather than claiming SQLite executes PostgreSQL branches.

The final broad review found one import regression: after UID and multiset replay matching, unmatched financial lines on an already complete B2B order were silently discarded while unrelated imported products committed. The importer now rejects that conflict and rolls back the whole operation. The original rollback test is retained and expanded to zero-value unmatched lines. Import, replay and internal transfer tests passed **45 tests with one PostgreSQL-specific skip** in 37.20 seconds.

A final fixture-only follow-up passed **34 tests in 39.38 seconds** across intents, connections and parity presence scans. Pending bills now have a future relative due date, packed cancellation checks the enforced graph while preserving the seller permission check at a valid pending edge, and the unreadable-table fault is restored before cleanup. Product behavior and completed-order terminal-state assertions were unchanged.

Remaining capabilities are explicit: AI status-only payment updates stay disabled, and standalone advances without invoice dues still need their own durable source and allocation/refund workflow. Historical inventory drift, unknown costs and ambiguous imported financial records require sourced reconciliation. This implementation does not claim those merchant records have already been repaired.
